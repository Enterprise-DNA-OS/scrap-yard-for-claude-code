# Why there is no front end

A scrap yard runs on a handful of records: sellers and their ID, weigh tickets, grades and the price board, what is on hold, what is in the yard, and what is sold forward. The screens on top of them are there because the database was hard to ask. It is not hard any more. Open this folder in Claude Code and ask "which copper can we bale this week?" or "are we going to fill the Harbour Metals contract?" and you get the answer from your own records.

## What you gain

- **Answers to your own questions**, not only the reports someone built.
- **No per-location fee.** Every yard and every person in the office can ask.
- **Your records in a database you own.** Plain tables. Back them up, query them from anything, leave any time.
- **The law checked before the money moves.** Cash, ID, vehicle identifiers and copper holds are checked when the ticket is written, not found at an inspection.

## What a screen gives that this does not

- **A weighbridge screen.** This does not read a scale. Weights are typed in or imported from the scale's own file. Enterprise DNA connects the scale for yards that want it.
- **Photos and ID scans at the counter.** This records that a photo was taken and the ID details, not the image. A counter app with a camera is a customisation.
- **Payments.** This records how a ticket was paid. It does not move money.
- **Drag and drop.** Tickets and loads change state by asking, not by dragging cards.

`npm run view` renders read-only pages (the week, stock and contracts, the price board) in your colours for anyone who wants to look rather than ask.
